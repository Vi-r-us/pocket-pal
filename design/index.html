The PocketPal design language

A living showcase of the tokens, surfaces, typography, and motion every PocketPal screen follows — built to feel modern, consistent, and trustworthy. Mirrors the real tokens in frontend/src/index.css. Tags marked to adopt are proposed in the spec and not yet in code.

01 — Foundations

Principles

Five rules that resolve every judgment call. When in doubt, optimize for clarity and consistency.

Clarity over decoration

One clear hero metric per screen. Supporting detail recedes in size and muted color. Visuals serve the data.

Trust through consistency

The same action looks and behaves the same everywhere. Predictability lowers cognitive load.

Accessibility is a foundation

WCAG AA contrast, full keyboard support, visible focus, and reduced-motion are built in, not bolted on.

Motion is functional

Every animation gives feedback, guides attention, shows a state change, or maps space. No purpose, no motion.

One product, one language

Auth, shell, dashboards, tables, and settings share the same tokens, surfaces, and motion.

05 — Foundations

Surfaces: glass vs. flat

Decide a surface's material by its density, not by the screen. This rule keeps auth and the app one product.

Glass

Shell · header · nav · KPI cards · modals · auth hero

Translucent over the radial backdrop. Never stack blur on tiny elements like icon tiles.

Flat (opaque card)

Tables · forms · settings panels

Dense, text-heavy surfaces stay opaque for maximum legibility over a busy background.

Iconography

size-4 · inline / buttons size-5 · nav / standalone lucide only · decorative icons get aria-hidden · icon-only controls get aria-label + tooltip
05b — Foundations

Liquid Glass to adopt

An Apple-style evolution of the frosted glass: layered translucency, a light-aware specular rim, and real edge refraction where the browser supports it. Compare the three materials below over a busy backdrop, and switch the theme in the header. Reserve it for chrome / KPI / modal surfaces — never behind dense data.

Your browser renders Tier 2 refraction: checking…

Current frosted .glass

Blur + tint + single top highlight. The baseline shipping material.

Tier 1 .liquid-glass

Layered fill, brightness lift, depth highlights, and a masked specular rim. Works in every browser.

Tier 2 .liquid-glass-refract

Adds SVG displacement so edges visibly refract the backdrop (Chromium). Falls back to Tier 1 elsewhere.

Guardrails: keep tables, forms, and dense numbers on opaque card — Liquid Glass never sits behind body text or data. Accessibility fallbacks are built in: under prefers-reduced-transparency or prefers-contrast: more the material drops to a near-opaque surface; under prefers-reduced-motion no fluid motion plays. Tier 2 is GPU-heavy — large, low-frequency surfaces only. Activating Tier 2 in the app needs the SVG #liquid-refraction filter mounted once at the app root.
